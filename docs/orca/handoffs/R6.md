# R6 handoff: extract `TrenchSystem`

> **In short:** `game/scripts/core/actors/trench_system.gd` (`TrenchSystem`) now owns trapping
> (trap on route arrival and on same-tick work completion), the hostile climb-out
> (escape_trench submission, activation, the `ticksRemaining` mirror, the exit rule and the
> blocked-exit retry), the rescue candidate and victim pools, the rescue target tiles and route
> rules, rescue retirement and completion, and the exposed rescue reasons. `WorldState`
> delegates to it. `RescueGiver` is now built on `TrenchSystem`'s callables, not on
> `WorldState` methods. Behaviour is unchanged. A trench/rescue scenario with eight mid-game
> save/load round trips produces the same saves, events, per-5-tick trace and `state_hash()`,
> byte for byte, as `main`, and so do the R1–R5 probes. `world_state.gd` drops from 3264 to
> 2995 lines.

**Move, not rewrite.** [`R6-move_check.py`](R6-move_check.py) takes each of the 18 moved
functions from `main`'s `world_state.gd`, applies the listed mechanical renames and compares
the result with the body in `trench_system.gd`, statement by statement, with comment and
blank lines dropped. For the four lifted blocks it takes the block from `main` (first to last
statement) and compares it with the helper's body, indentation ignored; the only statement
the helpers add is `trap_on_work_complete()`'s `return true` (standing for the block's
`return`) and its final `return false`. The renames are:

- the new names; `_rescue_status` → `rescue_status`, `_trap_tile_before` → `trap_tile_before`;
- `get_tile(`/`passability(` → `_tile_grid.`/`_passability.` (the `WorldState` methods were
  one-line delegations to exactly these);
- `_find_colonist`, `_find_job_colonist`, `_finish_job`, `_drop_carried_haul_item`,
  `_resolve_giver_association`, `_set_work_progress` → injected `Callable`s (`.call(`), with an
  explicit type on the `var x := …` lines that assigned their result (`var actor: Dictionary =`),
  because GDScript cannot infer a type from `Callable.call()`;
- `_rescue_giver.resolve_job(`/`.victim_for_job(`, `_need_giver.searching_colonist_ids()`/
  `.get_pending_assignments()`, `_combat_giver.owns(` → injected `Callable`s (same typing rule:
  `var victim_id: String =`, `var searching: Array[String] =`);
- `in _colonists:` → `in _get_colonists.call():`.

Result: `move_check: 18 function(s) + 4 block(s), 0 differ`. The checker is not vacuous: with
`victim["y"] = rescuer["x"]` in `complete_rescue()` and `target` instead of `trap_tile_before`
in `trap_on_work_complete()` it reports `2 differ` (tried, then restored).

**Dependencies (no back-reference).** `TrenchSystem.new(content, relations, tile_grid,
passability, scheduler, progress_table, clock, get_colonists, find_colonist, find_job_colonist,
finish_job, drop_carried_haul_item, resolve_giver_association, set_work_progress,
need_searching_ids, need_pending_assignments, combat_owns, rescue_victim_id_for_job,
resolve_rescue_job)` is built in `WorldState._init()` right after `ConstructionSystem`, before
`_build_dimension_services()` (which builds `RescueGiver` from its callables). Its arguments
are:

- sibling modules and services that `StateCodec.decode()` restores in place and never
  reassigns (`_tile_grid`, `_passability`, `_scheduler`, `_progress_table`, `_clock`), plus
  `_content` and `_relations`;
- a lambda returning `_colonists`, because `decode()` reassigns that field;
- six bound `WorldState` methods and `_combat_giver.owns` (built once in `_init()`);
- four lambdas over `_need_giver`/`_rescue_giver`, because `_build_dimension_services()`
  rebuilds both givers (again on `decode()`) after `TrenchSystem` exists.

It never stores or receives `WorldState`.

**`RescueGiver` depends on `TrenchSystem`.** Four of its constructor callables now come from
the module: `_trench.rescue_target_candidates`, `_trench.set_rescue_reason`,
`_trench.is_trench_tile` and `_trench.retire_rescue_job` (were the `WorldState` methods of the
same names). Its candidate and victim pools come from `_trench.rescue_candidates()` /
`_trench.trapped_rescue_victims()`. `rescue_giver.gd` changes only in doc comments (they now
name the `TrenchSystem` functions). The giver itself, `_rescue_giver`, stays a `WorldState`
field like every other giver, so `test_rescue.gd` (which reads `world._rescue_giver` 21 times)
and `state_codec.gd` are untouched.

**Kept in `WorldState` on purpose** (other tasks own them; receiver renames only):

- `_resume_paused_rescue()`: it is part of the need pause/resume path (`_resume_paused_job()`,
  R8) and not in the R6 list; it now calls `_trench.rescue_routable_to()` and
  `_trench.retire_rescue_job()`.
- `_toil_go_to_passable()`, `_toil_on_unreachable()`, `_toil_on_work_complete()` (R11): they call
  the module.
- `_advance_colonists()` and `_committed_jobs()` (R12): they set `_trench.trap_tile_before`, call
  `rescue_activation_safe()`/`retire_rescue_job()`/`check_trench_arrival()`, and merge
  RescueGiver's commitments as before.
- `_actor_may_reserve_target()` (the trapped-actor reservation gate of the scheduler) and
  `_remove_colonist_by_id()` (a trapped actor is not despawned): they only read `trapped`.
- `RESCUE_JOB_PRIORITY`, `REASON_NO_RESCUER_AVAILABLE` and `TILE_TRENCH` stay as public
  `WorldState` constants.
- The `rescueVictimAssignments` codec helpers stay in `StateCodec`: that is `RescueGiver`'s

## Contract impact

- No schema bump, migration, ADR or contract change.
- **Save format:** unchanged, and the codec is untouched. `TrenchSystem` owns no persisted
  state, so it has no `encode()`/`decode()`/`hash_into()`:
  - `trapped` lives on the actor record (`{tile}` for a colonist,
    `{tile, ticksRemaining, fromTile}` for a hostile actor) and is saved with the entities
    exactly as before (`StateCodec._decode_trapped_field()`);
  - the rescue job → victim associations are `RescueGiver`'s (`rescueVictimAssignments`), and
    the rescuer → job map is rebuilt from the scheduler on load, as before;
  - `rescue_status` and `trap_tile_before` were never saved and still are not; a loaded world
    starts with an empty reason cache, as before (`decode()` builds a new `WorldState`).
  The `save_200.json`/`save_400.json` and `states.json` below are byte-identical.
- **`state_hash()` coverage** (`R6-hash_coverage.gd`; `main` and the branch print the same 5
  lines):
  - Included (unchanged): an actor's `trapped` (colonist shape and hostile shape, through
    `colonists`), and `rescue_victim_assignments` (RescueGiver).
  - Deliberately excluded (unchanged): `rescue_status` and `trap_tile_before` (runtime only).
- **Invariants from trimmed comments** are in the `TrenchSystem` class doc: only a colonist or
  a colony-hostile actor is trapped; the two `trapped` shapes and their key order (hashed by
  `JSON.stringify()`); `ticksRemaining` mirrors the escape job's work timer and never counts
  down on its own; a rescue never targets nor is routed through a trench; the module owns no
  persisted state. Each function keeps a 1–5 line *why*; the longer rescue essays stay in
  `rescue_giver.gd`'s class doc, which is where the rule lives.

### Byte identity

[`R6-scenario_dump.gd`](R6-scenario_dump.gd) uses seed 777, a 48x48 map and incidents on. At
tick 0 it:

- clears a 7x7 yard at the map centre, moves the 3 spawned colonists there and adds 4 more;
- carves a vertical trench line east of the yard and a horizontal one south of it, and orders
  chop/till/mine/dig work on the far side, so colonists cross a trench, get trapped and are
  rescued by the others;
- sends four wolves (wildlife, hostile to the colony) across a trench with autonomous incident
  jobs, so they get trapped and climb out; one falls into a walled pit whose entry tile is then
  closed until tick 200, so its climb-out finds no exit and is retried.

The colonist roster is read from `world._colonists` (the live roster, wolves included; the
same field on both sides), not `get_colonists()`, which returns copies of workers only.

Then every 20 ticks it issues three random orders or a `cancel_job` on a live
rescue/escape_trench job, and every 120 ticks sends another wolf. Every command is previewed,
then applied. Every 100 ticks the world is saved (raw and through `SaveIO`), reloaded with
`from_save_state()`, and play continues on the reloaded copy. Every 5 ticks the probe traces
every actor's position, `trapped` and rescue reason, the raw rescue-reason cache,
`trap_tile_before`, the rescue/escape_trench jobs, RescueGiver's two association maps and
`state_hash()`.
