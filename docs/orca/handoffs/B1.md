# B1 handoff: large area orders freeze the game, selection lags the cursor

> **In short:** Measured first, then fixed in three steps, one commit each, as the owner's
> note asked. (1) Several core paths cost a full pass over every job or event per job, per
> command or per tick; they now cost what they return, with no behaviour change (saves, event
> logs, scheduler and `state_hash()` identical to `main`). (2) The viewer now previews a drag
> incrementally and draws designations and selections from small textures instead of one draw
> call per tile. (3) After 1 and 2 a whole-map release still took ~6 s (Dig) / ~12 s (Cancel)
> in one frame, so a dragged rectangle is now one `area_order` command that the core expands
> 128 tiles per tick, saved (schema 25 -> 26, ADR 044). A whole-map Dig release went from
> "hours" (extrapolated; 10,000 tiles took 317 s) to **9 ms**; a whole-map drag from **847 ms**
> per mouse move to **9 ms**; a tick with 8,446 queued jobs from **3,150 ms** to **3 ms**
> (58,612 jobs: 3.6 ms).

## Causes found, with numbers

All numbers: `bench_area_orders.gd`, 256x256, seed 42, on `main` `30a86d4` unless noted.

- **(b) apply was quadratic, in the core, not in the viewer.** `JobQueue._emit()` re-sorted
  its whole event log on every event: 3,000 `submit_dig` calls took 41 s on their own.
  `EventLog.collect_queue_events()` deep-copied the queue's whole log on every command, and
  `insert_event()` scanned back over every event of the current tick (a `command_applied` at
  priority 0 sorts before all of the tick's queue events). `GlobalAssignment.submit()`
  scanned `_waiting` linearly, as did every removal. Apply of 1,000 tiles: 2,533 ms; 10,000:
  316,678 ms; 65,536: extrapolated at ~13,600 s.
- **The tick after a big order** was the second freeze: `JobQueue._find_job()` scanned every
  job and the labour refresh called it once per waiting job (O(N^2) per tick); the haul and
  construction givers, `_active_site_fetch_job_ids()` and
  `_resolve_freshly_activated_site_fetch_sources()` each deep-copied every job every tick;
  `get_reservations()` walked every job twice. Steady tick with 8,446 queued jobs: 3,150 ms.
- **(a) the preview** re-validated the whole rectangle through `WorldState.preview()` on every
  mouse move: worst move 847 ms for a full-map drag (65,536 tiles at ~10 µs each); Cancel's
  preview additionally deep-copied every job *per tile* (1,000 tiles: 2,244 ms).
- **(c) drawing** redrew one rect per pending job and three draw calls per preview tile every
  redraw, and the stockpile pass walked every cell of every zone: a redraw costs 331 ms with
  58,612 pending jobs and 48 ms with a whole-map zone (draw-callback time, measured on the step
  1 commit, whose draw code is still `main`'s).
- **Per-tick viewer reads:** the info panels deep-copied every job (several times per colonist)
  and every event each tick: 280 ms / 481 ms per read at 58,612 jobs.

## What changed

**Step 1 (no behaviour change).** Indexes and tails instead of scans and full copies (table
above). Each index is valid only while `JobQueue._jobs` is the list it was built for:
`AssignmentQueue.advance_selection()` temporarily swaps in a subset, and every indexed read
then scans that subset exactly as before. Orders are preserved exactly: binary searches
insert at the index the old linear scans found (the arrays are sorted by the same
comparator, whose keys are unique), the event log is sorted on read by the same total order,
`get_events_after()` returns exactly the tail `get_events()` would list.

**Step 2 (viewer).** `drag_preview.gd`: for per-tile tools a drag validates only the tiles a
move newly covers (at most 1,024 now, the rest a budget per frame), keeps known validity for
the whole drag and refreshes stale tiles a budget per frame after a tick (a coarse preview;
release applies the exact rules); the fill is one pixel per map cell in a texture, outlines
and crosses only for on-screen tiles and only while there are at most 4,096. Wall, zone and
the hover/footprint previews keep their one-call path. The designation overlay is a texture
updated from `get_designation_changes()`, stacking alpha per job as one rect per job did
(`site_fetch` targets, which move, are still drawn per refresh). Cancel reads every job once
per rectangle (re-reading each candidate's live status). Stockpile counts visit only zoned
cells that hold items, in the same order.

**Step 3 (ADR 044).** Decision rule from the owner's note: after steps 1 and 2 a whole-map
release still cost 6,150 ms (Dig) and 11,694 ms (Cancel) in one frame, far above 100 ms. A
per-tile tool now submits one `area_order {order, x, y, width, height, priority}`; the core
expands it row-major into exactly the command a click submits, through `apply()`, 128 tiles
at acceptance and 128 at the start of each `tick()`. Cancel's pick (site first, else queued
before active, higher priority, lower id) moved from the viewer into
`AreaOrders.cancel_before()`, which the preview calls too, and only ever touches jobs/sites
older than the order. The queue is saved (`areaOrders`, last field, schema 26) and hashed
while pending. 128 rather than 256 per tick: 256 measured 21 ms (Dig) / 33 ms (Cancel) mean
per expansion tick, a dropped frame each tick.

## Known limitations

- **Owner confirmation on the native build is pending** (post-merge, non-blocking per
  REVIEW.md item 7): drag the whole map with Dig, Cancel and Zone, check the selection follows
  the cursor and nothing freezes.
- A large area order fills in over ticks: a whole 256x256 map takes 512 ticks, about 4 minutes
  at X1 (85 s at X3), while the colony already works the first rows. `TILES_PER_STEP` is one
  constant if the owner prefers a faster fill at a higher per-tick cost (256: ~21-33 ms ticks).
- Tiles of a large order are judged when expanded, not at release; the rejected-tiles text
  lists only tiles rejected at release, later ones are `command_rejected` events.
- The per-tile preview is coarse across ticks: after a tick, stale tiles keep their last colour
  until refreshed (1,024 per frame). Release always applies the exact rules.
- Expansion ticks still cost ~12-17 ms on average (worst ~25 ms) for 128 tile commands at the
  58k-job scale: each goes through the full `apply()` path (~70 µs of GDScript). A batch path
  would be ~3x cheaper per tile but a second rule path; ADR 044 records the choice.
- The wall tool and the zone tool still preview the whole rectangle per move (one call; the
  zone check is cheap, 8 ms at 65,536 tiles; a whole-map wall batch was not part of the
  symptom).
- `EventLog` and `JobQueue`'s event logs still grow without bound for the whole session
  (pre-existing); they no longer cost anything per command or tick, only memory.
