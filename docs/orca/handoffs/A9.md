# A9 handoff: viewer at 1,000 actors

> **In short:** One viewer refresh at 1,000 actors took about **415 ms** on `main`. On this
> branch it takes **3.1–5.0 ms** (mean per run; acceptance is ≤ 4 ms). **The acceptance is
> borderline:** on 2026-10-28 the means were 3.1–4.1 ms; on 2026-10-29, after a reboot and with
> the machine slower, this head's 12 runs average **4.29 ms** (3.58–5.01). An A/B run in the
> same session puts the previous head at 4.42 ms, so the code did not get slower; the margin
> is too thin to hold on a busier machine (see Validation and Known limitations).
>
> - The viewer reads the world once per refresh through a new read-only
>   `WorldState.get_view_snapshot(detail_ids)` (ADR 055). Before, it called several getters
>   that each deep-copied every colonist.
> - Colonist and actor sprites keep no node per actor: one `_draw()` per layer.
> - The colonist panel and the work table update their rows in place.
> - `TickDriver` runs at most 4 ticks per frame.
> - The purity rule now rejects `world._x`, with a planted read.
> - Suite: **121 of 121 PASS** on this head (`main`: 120).
> - Review round 1 (blocking: stale panel rows after a paused command) is fixed: a
>   same-tick refresh recomposes every row, and rows on screen are recomposed every tick.
> - Review round 2 (blocking: stale rows revealed by scrolling while paused) is fixed: rows
>   scrolled or resized into view are recomposed at once, with a regression check.

## What changed, goal by goal

1. **One read-only snapshot per tick** (`game/scripts/core/world/view_snapshot.gd`, new;
   `WorldState.get_view_snapshot(detail_ids)`; ADR 055).
   - **Why not one deep copy of every actor:** at 1,000 actors that costs about 9 ms, and
     freezing it about 11 ms more (measured). So the snapshot has two parts:
     - **columns:** one read-only `Array` per fact the map and the lists read for every actor
       (`ids`, `kinds`, `factions`, `x`, `y`, `worker`, `carrying`, `tool_kinds`, `trapped`,
       `work_kinds`, `hp`, `max_hp`, and `workers_by_id`, the sorted roster);
     - **details:** a frozen `get_colonists()`-shaped copy of only the colonists in
       `detail_ids`, with their jobs, pending need jobs and rescue reasons.
   - It also holds one copy each of assignments, the queued-job summary, tool items, items,
     construction sites, zones, trade offers and need reasons.
   - Every `Dictionary` and `Array` in it is read-only. `test_viewer_snapshot.gd` found that
     a `PackedInt32Array` column is shared by reference: a viewer write reached the snapshot.
     That is why the columns are `Array`s.
   - The module never holds `WorldState`. It gets the live actor table, the content registry,
     two live read-only views (`job_kinds`, `need_status`) and three getters as `Callable`s.
   - It reads `held_tool` through `WorkerType.get_held_tool()` and `route` through
     `ActorTable.get_component(actor, "mover")`, as `test_actor_field_lint.gd` requires.
   - `boot.gd._refresh()` takes one snapshot, asking for the colonist panel's window of rows
     (`InfoPanel.detail_ids()`). It passes that snapshot to `map_view`, both panels and the
     need alerts.
2. **Batched actors** (`viewer/sprite_record.gd`, new; `colonist_sprites.gd`;
   `actor_sprites.gd`).
   - A colonist was an `AnimatedSprite2D` plus three `Sprite2D` badges plus two `ColorRect`
     health-bar parts. It is now plain `SpriteRecord`s (the same fields: `position`,
     `animation`, `frame`, `flip_h`, `visible`, texture/region/scale/modulate).
   - One `_draw()` paints the layers in order: bodies, cargo badges, tool badges, trapped
     badges, health bars. Wolves, raiders and traders work the same way in `actor_sprites.gd`.
   - When the actor table has the same rows as last refresh, a colonist whose tile, cargo,
     tool, trapped flag and work kind did not change is skipped. Its records already show
     that, and `advance()` keeps the glide moving.
3. **Panels update rows** (`colonist_panel.gd`, `work_table_panel.gd`, `info_panel.gd`).
   - **Colonist panel.** Rows are kept by id. A label's text is set only when its line
     changed. Rows are added, removed or reordered only when the roster or the trade offers
     change.
   - **Which rows are recomposed.** On a tick: the rows on screen (inside the enclosing
     `ScrollContainer`, found by binary search since rows sit in roster order), at most
     `ROWS_PER_REFRESH` (16) more in a window that walks the id-sorted roster, and any new or
     rebuilt row. A colony of 16 or fewer sees every line every tick, as before.
   - **After a command.** A refresh at the same tick as the last one did not come from a
     tick: it follows a command or a world swap, also while paused. It recomposes every row,
     and `detail_ids()` asks the snapshot for every colonist's record. Review round 1.
   - **Scrolling or resizing.** The panel listens to its `ScrollContainer` (scroll bar
     `value_changed` and `resized`, deferred until the container has moved its content).
     Every row then on screen whose line was composed at an older tick than the world's is
     recomposed at once, while running or paused (`refresh_rows_on_screen()`, which tracks
     each row's tick in `_composed_at`). Review round 2.
   - **Recruit check.** It now reads the columns once per refresh. Before, it called
     `get_colonists()` once per row (N² copies).
   - **Work table.** It compares a row's `labourTable` only for the colonists the snapshot
     details, and touches its cells only when the table differs. Its own clicks refresh that
     row at once. A rebuild (the roster changed) or a refresh without a snapshot reads every
     row.
4. **`TickDriver` caps catch-up** (`viewer/tick_driver.gd`). `MAX_TICKS_PER_FRAME = 4`. Past
   it, the rest of the backlog is dropped and only the fractional part is kept. The game then
   runs slower than the chosen speed after a slow frame, never in a burst.
5. **Viewer purity** (`tests/test_architecture_rules.gd`).
   - New rule `_private_world_access()`: no `<name containing "world">._member` read or write.
     It scans every `viewer/*.gd` and `boot.gd`.
   - The existing call rule was moved into `_viewer_call_violations()`, with its regex and
     allow-list unchanged.
   - Both are run on a planted source: `world._tiles` and `new_world._items[...] = 1` must
     give 2 violations, and `world.free_tile()` 1. A clean source (getters, `apply()`, an
     unrelated `_world_label`) must give none.

- **`test_viewer_snapshot.gd` checks:**
  - **Getters.** On a debug scenario 30 ticks in, plus a wolf: every column agrees with
    `get_colonists()`, `get_actors_with_health()`, `get_job()` and
    `get_colonist_need_reason()`. Each detail equals its `get_colonists()` entry. Jobs,
    assignments, items, zones and sites equal their getters. An unknown or empty
    `detail_ids` gives no details.
  - **Read-only copy.** Every container is read-only, and every column is a read-only
    `Array`. Writing to a column's packed copy leaves the snapshot and the world unchanged.
    20 more ticks leave an earlier snapshot unchanged.
  - **No nodes.** Both sprite layers have **0** child nodes, and there is one body record per
    colonist.
  - **Panel window.** 37 colonists (several working till orders), all labels set to
    "stale". Each tick refresh recomposes at most 16 × (refresh number) lines, and all rows
    are fresh after ceil(37 / 16) = 3 ticks. The first row is the same node throughout.
  - **Paused command (review round 1).** After a tick refresh, `cancel_job` is applied at the
    same tick to a working colonist outside the next window. `detail_ids()` then names every
    colonist; the refresh recomposes every row; the colonist's row changes and equals
    `_line_for()` of its new state. On the code before the fix this row stayed stale.
  - **Rows on screen.** The panel sits in a 300 px `ScrollContainer` in the tree, with real
    frames, scrolled to 2,000 px. Some on-screen rows lie outside the window; after one tick
    refresh every on-screen row is recomposed, and nothing else beyond the window.
  - **Scrolling while paused (review round 2).** Then, with no tick and no command, the view is
    scrolled to a row near the end. Every row it reveals shows `_line_for()` of the current
    state and is marked composed at the current tick; none is left with the "stale" marker.
    With the scroll listener disabled, this check fails (4 revealed rows stale).
  - **`TickDriver` cap.** A 10 s frame at x3 (60 ticks due) runs exactly 4. The next frame
    runs none of the backlog. A 0.2 s frame then runs 1. The world's tick matches the count.

## Known limitations

- **The ≤ 4 ms acceptance is not reliably met.** On 2026-10-28 the mean met it in 11 of 12
  runs (3.1–4.1 ms). On 2026-10-29 the same code, on a slower-running machine, averaged
  4.29 ms over 12 runs (4 of 12 at or under 4 ms); p95 is 5.3–8.6 ms. The A/B run shows the
  code did not regress; the margin is simply about 10%, smaller than this machine's
  day-to-day spread. Whether that meets "≤ 4 ms per tick" is the owner's call; if not, the
  next step is a follow-up that cuts the snapshot's columns (below), not a change here.
  - About 2.4 ms of each refresh is the snapshot's columns. That is roughly 1 µs per actor
    of GDScript dictionary reads; it cannot shrink much more without core-side change
    tracking.
- **At 1,000 colonists a row off screen can be up to 63 ticks old** (ceil(1000 / 16); about
  31 s at x1) while the game runs without commands.
  - Rows on screen are recomposed every tick, every row after any command, and rows scrolled
    into view at once (also while paused). Off-screen staleness is therefore never visible.
  - Colonies of 16 or fewer are unaffected.
  - New, removed and rebuilt rows (the Recruit button appearing) are immediate.
- **A command at 1,000 colonists costs about 33 ms of refresh** (every row recomposed; mean of
  10 same-tick `boot._refresh()` calls, measured with a throwaway script). A tick refresh is
  unaffected. The headless bench has no layout, so its numbers include no on-screen rows; in
  the game the few rows on screen each add one line's composition (not measured here).
- **Draw order.** Badges now stack above every colonist body, and health bars above every
  badge. Before, each colonist's nodes were interleaved in creation order. The difference
  shows only where two colonists overlap on screen.
- **Swapping in a 1,000-colonist world** builds the rows once (about 0.8 s). This cost is
  unchanged from `main`'s 0.99 s swap.
- **"Manual run shows the same screen" is owner-pending**: it needs the native build.
  - The headless tests check the same positions, frames, badges, bars and panel lines as
    before.
  - No capture was taken: the capture tools need a display.
